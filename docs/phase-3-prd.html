1. Goal and users

Give Ops and Finance a trusted, early view of which Risk contracts are pacing below target, so they can act before the contract expires. Success is judged by the metrics set on the opportunity page: detection lead time first, with false-positive, alert-volume and reconciliation guardrails.

Ops · Maria

Works contract by contract. Needs to know each morning which contracts to open first and why, while there is still time to reprice, switch channel or move assets into an auction.

Finance · David

Works at book level. Needs exposure at risk by region, channel, asset type and consignor, instead of a backward-looking quarterly report.

Data · Priya

Builds today's one-off views by hand. Needs contracts, listings and sales joined and reconciled, so a report becomes a query.

2. Scope

Pilot
  • Linear pacing and the risk flag
  • Projected shortfall and exposure at risk
  • Daily batch refresh with cross-source reconciliation
  • Ranked daily list of flagged contracts for Ops
  • Book rollups by region, channel, asset type and consignor for Finance
  • Natural-language questions over these metrics
Next
  • Pacing against the historical sales curve of similar past contracts, by asset type, channel and region
  • Suggested interventions per flagged contract
  • Logging of Ops actions, so the recovery rate can be measured instead of assumed

Out of scope for both: automated repricing or channel moves, real-time streaming, what-if simulation of moving assets between channels, and optimizing the upside tiers.

Each requirement says what the product must do and who it is for, not how to build it. The how is settled with Engineering in Phase 4, and every backlog story names the DPR it satisfies.

DPR-1

Today contracts, listings and sales live in three systems and an analyst is the integration layer.

Key entities

The requirements assume this conceptual model. Its physical design, keys and grain are the first story in the backlog.

Consignor → Contract → Asset → Sale event  ·  dimensions: channel (app, website, live), region

A contract carries its target price, its 90-day window and its tier schedule, which can be negotiated per contract. Each asset belongs to one contract. Each sale event records the channel and region it happened in.

4. Governed metric definitions

These are the definitions the semantic layer in Phase 6 implements and the conversational layer in Phase 7 queries. They are written once here so no later phase reinvents them.

MetricDefinitionGrain
Elapsed sharedays_elapsed ÷ lifecycle_days, capped at 1Contract, daily
Realized salesSum of gross sale proceeds to date for the contract's assetsContract, daily
Pacing %realized ÷ (target × elapsed_share)Contract, daily
Risk flag (pilot)Pacing % below 80%, on an active contract, from day 15 onward. The first two weeks are excluded because a single early sale or its absence swings pacing too much to mean anything.Contract, daily
Projected final valuerealized ÷ elapsed_share (linear projection)Contract, daily
Projected shortfallmax(0, target − projected_final)Contract, daily
Exposure at riskSum of projected shortfall across flagged contractsAny rollup: region, channel, asset type, consignor
Detection lead timeexpiry_date − first_flag_date for contracts closing below target; 0 if never flaggedContract, at close

A worked example

A $50,000 contract on a 90-day window has sold $11,000 by day 30. Linear pacing expects a third of the target by then, about $16,700, so pacing is 66% and the contract is flagged. Projected linearly, it finishes near $33,000, a projected shortfall of about $17,000.

On this contract's actual sales path, the flag first fires on day 15, the first day it is allowed to. Today it would be noticed around day 85. That difference, 75 days of lead time against about 5, is what the primary metric measures.

The questions these metrics must answer

Each of the four questions validated in discovery maps onto the definitions above, so DPR-9 can answer all of them without a new metric.

Question from discoveryAnswered with
How are a consignor's contracts performing?Pacing % and exposure at risk, by consignor
Which asset types underperform, and in which channel?Pacing % by asset type and channel
Which contracts are close to expiry and still behind?Risk flag, filtered by days to expiry
Do some regions or channels have performance issues?Share of contracts flagged and exposure at risk, by region and channel

5. Non-functional requirements

IDRequirement
NFR-1Daily batch refresh, landed before the Ops day starts, with all three sources taken from the same point in time.
NFR-2One definition per metric. Lists, rollups and answers to questions all read the governed metrics; none recomputes them.
NFR-3Reproducible by date. Any day's output can be regenerated for that as-of date and comes out the same.
NFR-4Read-only access for questions. The question layer can't change data and can't query raw source tables directly.

Why daily

Discovery left open how fresh the signal needs to be. David can work with weekly, Priya heard stakeholders ask for near-real-time, and she would trade speed for consistency. Tying cadence to the decision it supports settles it.

The decision is Ops choosing to reprice, switch channel or move a contract into an auction, and it plays out over days. A weekly refresh could spend up to 7 of the days the primary metric is trying to win. Real-time adds cost and makes it harder to keep the three source systems consistent, which is Priya's essential requirement. A daily batch refresh keeps every system on the same snapshot and gives Maria her morning list.

6. Release criteria

Before Ops relies on the flag, the backtest (DPR-7) replays it over the last 12 months of closed contracts, whose final outcomes are already known. The pilot goes live with Ops when, on the backtest:

  • median detection lead time is at least 30 days,
  • at least 70% of underperformers are flagged by day 45,
  • no more than 40% of flags are false alarms,
  • new flags stay at or below 25 a week, and
  • every active contract reconciles across the three sources.

The 80% threshold and the day-15 floor are starting values, not commitments. The backtest sweeps both and picks the setting that best meets the lead-time target while staying inside the guardrails.

7. Known limitations, assumptions and dependencies

Limitations of the pilot

  • Back-loaded contracts look worse than they are. A contract whose assets are scheduled into a live auction late in the window will pace low until the auction and then catch up. Linear pacing will flag it. This is the main reason historical-curve pacing comes next, and the false-positive rate by channel will show how large the effect is.
  • Single-asset contracts are all or nothing. A contract covering one high-value item shows $0 realized until it sells, so pacing says little about it. False positives by contract size will show whether this segment needs separate handling.
  • The backtest can't prove the dollars. Payouts avoided depend on Ops acting on a flag, which history can't show. The pilot proves the signal; logging Ops actions, planned next, is what proves the dollars.

Assumptions and dependencies

  • Twelve months of contract, listing and sales history can be extracted from the three source systems.
  • Ops commits to working the morning list during the pilot, so live lead time reflects the flag and not whether anyone looked.
  • Contract targets and windows in ConsignTrack are accurate; the product reports on them but doesn't validate the commercial terms.

8. Open questions for Engineering

These are the questions the sizing session in Phase 4 needs to settle before estimates mean anything.

  1. Extraction. Do we take full daily snapshots of the three sources or capture changes, and how do we freeze them to the same point in time (NFR-1)?
  2. Source of truth. When systems disagree on a contract's status, which one wins for the flag, while DPR-2 still reports the disagreement?
  3. Listings. Does the pilot need listings modeled as their own entity, to show where an asset was placed over time, or is channel on the sale event enough?
  4. Delivery. Where does Maria's morning list live: a dashboard, an email, or the question interface?
  5. Day boundary. Which time zone defines a contract day, for the day-15 floor and for expiry?
  6. Question layer. What hosting and cost envelope does DPR-9 need, and how is its accuracy tested before Ops sees it?
