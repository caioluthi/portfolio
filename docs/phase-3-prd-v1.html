Phase 3 · Product requirements

What the contract-risk product must do, written down once so Engineering can size it.

Second Avenue is a fictional consignment retailer and every figure on this page is simulated. This page says what the product must do, with a one-line reason for each requirement. Why it matters, how success is judged and what the pilot is betting on are on the opportunity page. Phase 4 prototypes the riskiest part of it. How the production build is done and when it ships come from Engineering in Phase 5.

TL;DR

Goal
Flag Risk contracts pacing below target a median of 30+ days before expiry, inside the false-alarm guardrails.
Pilot
Linear pacing, a daily risk flag, exposure at risk, a ranked list for Ops, rollups for Finance and plain-language questions on top.
Requirements
Nine data product requirements, DPR-1 to DPR-9: seven must-haves and two should-haves, with the required and calculated fields each one depends on. Every story in the backlog traces to one of them.
Next
Phase 4 tests the pilot bet with a prototype built against these requirements. Phase 5 takes the revised page to an Engineering sizing session and breaks it into epics and stories.

1. Goal and users

Give Ops and Finance a trusted, early view of which Risk contracts are pacing below target, so they can act before the contract expires. Success is judged by the metrics set on the opportunity page: detection lead time first, with false-positive, alert-volume and reconciliation guardrails.

Ops · Maria

Works contract by contract. Needs to know each morning which contracts to open first and why, while there is still time to reprice, switch channel or move assets into an auction.

Finance · David

Works at book level. Needs exposure at risk by region, channel, asset type and consignor, instead of a backward-looking quarterly report.

Data · Priya

Builds today's one-off views by hand. Needs contracts, listings and sales joined and reconciled, so a report becomes a query.

2. Scope

Pilot
  • Linear pacing and the risk flag
  • Projected shortfall and exposure at risk
  • Daily refresh with cross-source reconciliation
  • Ranked daily list of flagged contracts for Ops
  • Book rollups by region, channel, asset type and consignor for Finance
  • Natural-language questions over these metrics
Next
  • Pacing against the historical sales curve of similar past contracts, by asset type, channel and region
  • Suggested interventions per flagged contract
  • Logging of Ops actions, so the recovery rate can be measured instead of assumed

Out of scope for both: automated repricing or channel moves, real-time streaming, what-if simulation of moving assets between channels, and optimizing the upside tiers.

3. Data product requirements

Each requirement says what the product must do and who it is for, not how to build it. The how is settled with Engineering in Phase 5, and every backlog story names the DPR it satisfies.

DPR-1
One contract view across systemsEvery Risk contract, active or closed, is joined with its consignor, its assets and their sales into a single daily snapshot that carries every required field.For Priya and everyone downstream. Today contracts, listings and sales live in three systems and an analyst is the integration layer.
Must
DPR-2
Cross-source reconciliationAt every refresh, each active contract's status and sales are checked across ConsignTrack, Listings and Sales. Disagreements are reported with the contract and the systems involved, never silently resolved.Priya's one essential requirement, and the reconciliation guardrail (100%).
Must
DPR-3
Governed metrics as calculated fieldsEvery calculated field is defined once, computed daily per contract from the required fields, and exposed by name to everything downstream. The flag threshold and the day floor are parameters, so they can change without changing a definition.The single source of the numbers Maria acts on, David reports and the success metrics judge. Nothing later in the build reinvents them.
Must
DPR-4
Exposure rollups for FinanceExposure at risk and the share of contracts flagged roll up by region, channel, asset type and consignor, from contract level to the whole book.For David. Replaces the quarterly Risk Book Summary as the view of exposure.
Must
DPR-5
Ranked morning list for OpsEach morning, Ops sees the flagged contracts ranked by projected shortfall, with pacing %, days to expiry and sales so far by channel, before the working day starts.For Maria, who described wanting a short list of which contracts to open first.
Must
DPR-6
Flag historyEach contract's daily flag state is kept, so its first flag date is known and any past day's list can be reproduced as it was.Detection lead time can't be calculated without it, and Finance needs to audit what was known when.
Must
DPR-7
BacktestThe daily flag can be replayed over the last 12 months of closed contracts, alongside today's practice of a manual review in the final week, producing every success metric. It can sweep the threshold and the day floor.The release criteria depend on it.
Must
DPR-8
Success scorecardOnce live, the success metrics are reported weekly from their calculated fields, broken out by channel and contract size.Keeps the guardrails honest after launch, and measures the two known risks to the pilot.
Should
DPR-9
Questions in plain languageOps and Finance can ask the four discovery questions in plain language. Answers come only from the calculated fields, name the metric and the as-of date, and say plainly when a question is outside what the product supports.For Maria and David, and what Priya called a nice-to-have: "then they'd stop asking me."
Should

Key entities

Consignor → Contract → Asset → Sale event  ·  dimensions: channel (app, website, live), region

A contract carries its target price, its 90-day window and its tier schedule, which can be negotiated per contract. Each asset belongs to one contract. Each sale event records the channel and region it happened in. The physical design, keys and grain are the first story in the backlog.

A worked example

A $50,000 contract on a 90-day window has sold $11,000 by day 30. Linear pacing expects a third of the target by then, about $16,700, so pacing is 66% and the contract is flagged. Projected linearly, it finishes near $33,000, a projected shortfall of about $17,000.

On this contract's actual sales path, the flag first fires on day 15, the first day it is allowed to. Today it would be noticed around day 85. That difference, 75 days of lead time against about 5, is what the primary metric measures.

The questions these metrics must answer

Each of the four questions validated in discovery maps onto the calculated fields above, so DPR-9 can answer all of them without a new metric.

4. Non-functional requirements

IDRequirement
NFR-1Daily refresh, landed before the Ops day starts, with all three sources reflecting the same point in time.
NFR-2One definition per metric. Lists, rollups and answers to questions all read the governed metrics; none recomputes them.
NFR-3Reproducible by date. Any day's output can be regenerated for that as-of date and comes out the same.
NFR-4Read-only access for questions. The question layer can't change data and can't query raw source tables directly.

Daily is the slowest refresh that doesn't spend the lead time the product exists to win; the reasoning is on the opportunity page.

5. Release criteria

The pilot goes live with Ops when the backtest meets the go-live criteria set on the opportunity page. The requirement on this side is DPR-7: the backtest must produce every number those criteria use, for any setting of the flag parameters.

6. Assumptions and dependencies

  • Twelve months of contract, listing and sales history can be extracted from the three source systems.
  • Ops commits to working the morning list during the pilot, so live lead time reflects the flag and not whether anyone looked.
  • Contract targets and windows in ConsignTrack are accurate; the product reports on them but doesn't validate the commercial terms.

The known risks to the pilot, and why linear pacing comes first, are on the opportunity page.

7. Open questions for Engineering

These are the questions that need settling before estimates mean anything. The prototype in Phase 4 tests the product decisions among them, such as channel attribution; the rest go to the Engineering sizing session in Phase 5.

  1. Extraction. Do we take full daily snapshots of the three sources or capture changes, and how do we freeze them to the same point in time (NFR-1)?
  2. Source of truth. When systems disagree on a contract's status, which one wins for the flag, while DPR-2 still reports the disagreement?
  3. Listings. Does the pilot need listings modeled as their own entity, to show where an asset was placed over time, or is channel on the sale event enough?
  4. Delivery. Where does Maria's morning list live: a dashboard, an email, or the question interface?
  5. Day boundary. Which time zone defines a contract day, for the day-15 floor and for expiry?
  6. Channel attribution. Pacing and exposure are contract-level, but a contract can sell across several channels. For rollups by channel, do we attribute a contract to the channel with most of its listed assets, or split its exposure by asset? This is a product decision to settle with Finance; Engineering needs to know which, because one needs listing history and the other doesn't.
  7. Question layer. What hosting and cost envelope does DPR-9 need, and how is its accuracy tested before Ops sees it?
