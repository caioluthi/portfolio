Phase 5 · Backlog & roadmap

Plan the production build from what the prototype learned.

Second Avenue is a fictional consignment retailer and every figure on this page is simulated. The backlog is planned from the revised PRD, and every story names the requirement it satisfies.

TL;DR

Why
The prototype tested the product bet. What is left is data and delivery risk: real source systems, a key field that isn't clean today, and a go-live test that has only run on simulated data.
What
Six epics, each an outcome someone can sign off on, with two or three functional stories each. Fifteen stories in all, traced to the requirements.
Output
A backlog refined with the Tech Lead, Engineering's sprint plan, and a Now / Next / Later roadmap built around the two decisions that can move the date.
Result
Three releases instead of one at week 10. The contract book in week 4 ends the manual report and shows the losses already locked in, without depending on the flag. A Midwest pilot follows in week 7, as soon as the flag passes its test on real history, and the full pilot in week 10. If last year's appraisals can't be recovered, the pilot moves to about week 20, but the contract book doesn't.

1. From prototype to production

The prototype answered the product questions: which flag works, with which settings, and what Ops and Finance need to see. What the production build still has to prove is different.

The prototype proved
  • A projection that counts unsold inventory flags underperformers a median of 30 days early, with 36% false alarms
  • The pilot settings, chosen by a rule fixed before the run
  • How exposure counts by channel: split over each contract's unsold items
  • What Ops and Finance need on their views, from the feedback sessions
Production still has to prove
  • The three real source systems can be landed, joined and reconciled every day
  • An intake appraisal exists for every item. Today repricing overwrites it
  • The flag holds on real history, not only on a simulated book
  • The product can be run and relied on: an owner, freshness, monitoring, access

Three choices shape the backlog:

  1. Milestones, not a feature list. Epics are grouped by the decision each milestone unlocks: does it hold on real data, then pilot live with Ops and Finance, then self-serve questions. That grouping is the roadmap.
  2. The prototype is a reference, not code to ship. Product hands over the metric definitions, the worked example as acceptance tests, and the prototype's queries as a reference. How it is built is Engineering's call.
  3. What the prototype skipped is first-class work. Landing, reconciliation, reproducibility and the production wrapper were deliberately left out of the prototype. Here they are epics and stories, so they aren't cut as overhead.

3. Stories

Two or three functional stories per epic, written as outcomes. Product writes who it's for, why, and what done looks like. Engineering breaks each story into technical tasks in refinement, in section 4.

Each story links to its GitHub issue, with epics as parent issues and releases as milestones. Persona names are from discovery: Maria in Ops, David in Finance, Priya in Data. Refinement changed E1-S1, E3-S1 and E4-S1 and added E4-S3; the versions shown here are after it.

E1 · Trusted contract data3 storiesNow

One daily, reconciled, reproducible snapshot of every contract, its assets and sales.

E1-S1 · #2

One daily snapshot of every contract

Priya · DPR-1, NFR-1, NFR-3

So that a report becomes a query instead of an eight-hour project.

Done when

  • Every Risk contract, active or closed, is joined with its consignor, assets and sales, and carries every required field.
  • The three sources reflect the same point in time and land before the Ops day starts. A missing source fails the run instead of landing partial data.
  • Any day's snapshot, from the first day of landing, can be regenerated and comes out the same.
E1-S2 · #3

A trustworthy intake appraisal

Critical pathPriya · NFR-5

So that the flag's key input can't be overwritten or quietly go missing.

Done when

  • The intake appraisal is its own field that repricing can't change, and lots appraised as one line are handled by one rule, the same way every time.
  • Intake values for the last 12 months are recovered from ConsignTrack's change history. If coverage is below 95%, the backtest falls back to a shadow period on new contracts, and the decision is recorded on the roadmap.
  • Every refresh reports appraisal coverage, and contracts below 95% are marked.
E1-S3 · #4

Reconciliation across the three systems

Priya · DPR-2

So that nobody acts on a number only one system believes.

Done when

  • Every active contract is marked reconciled or not on every refresh, and the day's rate is one published number.
  • Each disagreement shows the contract, the field and the value in each system. Nothing is silently overwritten.
  • One documented rule decides which status the flag uses when systems disagree.
E2 · Governed risk metrics3 storiesNow

The projection, flag and exposure defined once, with daily flag history. The PRD's worked example is the acceptance test throughout.

E2-S1 · #6

A daily projection and risk flag per contract

Maria · DPR-3

So that falling-behind contracts reach me while there's still time to act.

Done when

  • The projection, flag and tier match their PRD definitions. In the worked example the contract projects to about $40,800 by day 30, goes on watch on day 21 and moves to act on day 22.
  • Flag settings and sell-through rates are parameters: changing them changes the results, not a definition or code.
  • Sell-through rates are re-learned from closed contracts on a schedule, and every run records which version it used.
E2-S2 · #7

Exposure and the facts Ops acts on

David, Maria · DPR-3

So that both views read the same numbers.

Done when

  • Exposure at risk counts act contracts only, exposure locked in covers contracts with nothing left to sell, and shortfall is split over unsold items by appraised value.
  • Each contract shows whether a live auction is still possible and how much unsold value sits on a weak channel for its item type.
E2-S3 · #8

Flag history and evaluation fields

David · DPR-6, NFR-2

So that lead time and the other success metrics can be measured at all.

Done when

  • Each contract's flag and tier are kept per day, append-only, so any past morning list can be rebuilt.
  • First act date, lead time and false positive match the PRD. In the worked example the lead time is 68 days.
  • Every calculated field is in one catalog under its PRD name, and nothing downstream recomputes one.
E3 · Go-live backtest2 storiesNow · gate

The pilot settings were chosen on a simulated book. Real history is a fresh test of that choice, and its result decides go-live.

E3-S1 · #10

Replay the pilot flag against today's practice

David · DPR-7

So that we know how much earlier it would have caught last year's underperformers.

Done when

  • The last 12 months are rebuilt as they stood on each day, and every day is replayed from that view, never from later data, beside a baseline where contracts are noticed in the final week.
  • The pilot settings stay fixed. The full settings sweep is reported, but any change to the settings goes back to Product.
E3-S2 · #11

Go-live report

David · DPR-7

So that the go-live decision is made on evidence, not opinion.

Done when

  • Each go-live criterion is shown as met or not met, with its value, beside the prototype's simulated result.
  • False alarms are broken out by channel and by contract size, and the appraisal coverage of the contracts tested is shown.
  • The report regenerates with one command.

4. Refinement with the Tech Lead

Tomás leads the Data Platform team: two data engineers and an analytics engineer, in two-week sprints. He read the PRD and the stories before the session, and we went through them in milestone order. His job in the room was the how and the when; mine was to answer what each story is for and to make the product calls only Product can make. Like the interviews, the session is simulated.

"Most of this I can plan today. The two things I can't are the ones that aren't ours: the ConsignTrack change and the data contracts."

Session notes

Where would you start?

"E1-S2. Before we commit to anything, I want a three-day spike on ConsignTrack's change history. If it keeps every edit to the estimate, recovery is a query. If it doesn't, your fallback kicks in, and I'd rather find that out in week one than in week six."

→ The spike goes first, and its result is the first gate on the roadmap.

Can E1-S1 hold up as written?

"Not the last criterion. We can make every snapshot reproducible from the day we start landing them, but we can't reproduce snapshots we never took. For the last 12 months we rebuild the view as it stood each day, once, for the backtest. That's a different job, and it belongs with the backtest."

→ Accepted. E1-S1 is reproducible from first landing; the 12-month rebuild moves into E3-S1.

How do you want to take the three sources?

"Full daily snapshots of all three, cut at the same timestamp. At a few thousand contracts it's cheap, and snapshots that never change give you reproducibility for free. We'd move to change capture only if volume grows."

When systems disagree on status, which one wins?

"That one's yours. Tell me and I'll build it."

→ Product's call: ConsignTrack, the contract system of record. Disagreements are still reported.

Which time zone defines a contract day?

"Pacific, since that's where ConsignTrack sets expiry dates. But check it with Ops. It's a commercial date, not a technical one."

→ Pacific by default, to confirm with Maria.

And lots appraised as one line?

"Treat a lot as one item for the pilot. Splitting it means parsing the appraisers' notes, and that's a project of its own."

→ Accepted, with one condition: the completeness report shows how much appraised value sits in lots, so we see if the rule starts to matter.

Where should Maria's morning list live?

"Not the question interface; that's later. The Risk Book Summary is already in Tableau, so put the list there, and send a morning email with the top of the act tier and a link."

Is the prototype useful to you?

"As a spec, yes. We'll port the logic and use your worked example as the first test. The queries themselves don't ship."

Ten weeks before anyone uses it is long. Can something ship earlier?

"The contract book is cheap. It's mostly E1, which we build in the first two sprints anyway: a view and a page on top. The Midwest email is fine too, it's a query on tables we already have. What I won't do is pull the Tableau work into sprint 3. That competes with the backtest, and the backtest is the gate."

→ The book ships in week 4 and the Midwest email right after the gate, in week 7. Tableau and the other regions stay in sprint 5. My trade: the Midwest pilot runs before the data contracts are signed, so Priya checks its reconciliation by hand.

What worries you?

"E5-S1. Data contracts with three other teams are mostly meetings. I can plan our side, not theirs. Start those conversations now, even if the epic is Next."

→ I open the data contract conversations in sprint 1.

The roadmap

Product

The two decisions that can move the date

◆ 1 · Week 1

Can last year's appraisals be recovered?

Pass
At least 95% of items on last year's contracts have a recovered intake appraisal. The backtest runs on real history.
Fail
The flag runs in the background on new contracts from sprint 3, when the new field ships, until a full 90-day window has closed. Both pilots move to about week 20. The contract book still ships in week 4.
Why first
It's the cheapest test of the assumption that moves the date the most.
◆ 2 · Week 6

Does the flag hold on real history?

Pass
The go-live report shows every criterion met. The Midwest pilot starts in week 7.
Fail
The settings come back to Product. If another setting meets every guardrail, I decide whether to switch. If none does, no pilot starts, listing-activity signals move up from Next scope, and the contract book carries on.
Why here
No prediction reaches Ops until the flag is proven. The contract book, which predicts nothing, ships before it.
