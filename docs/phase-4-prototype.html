Second Avenue 1 · Discovery 2 · Opportunity 3 · PRD 4 · Prototype

Phase 4 · Prototype & validation · In progress

Before asking Engineering to build it, test whether the flag works.

Second Avenue is a fictional consignment retailer and every figure on this page is simulated. The prototype is built against the PRD and judged by the go-live criteria set on the opportunity page.

TL;DR

Why
The whole product rests on one bet: a simple pacing flag catches failing contracts weeks earlier without burying Ops in false alarms. That is cheap to test and expensive to assume.
What
A timeboxed, throwaway prototype: the governed metrics on a simulated book of contracts, a backtest against last year, Ops and Finance views, and plain-language questions on top.
Output
A go-live verdict, chosen flag settings, the failure cases sized, and a revised PRD.
Next
Phase 5 plans the production build from the revised PRD, with Engineering.

1. What the prototype must answer

A prototype is worth building only if it answers questions that a document can't. These six decide whether the pilot goes ahead, how it is configured, and what changes in the requirements.

#QuestionEvidence it produces
Q1Does the bet hold? Does a linear pacing flag meet the five go-live criteria on last year's contracts?A backtest against today's practice, a review in the final week, with each criterion met or not met
Q2What should the flag settings be?A sweep of threshold and day floor that replaces the 80% and day-15 starting values
Q3Where does linear pacing fail?False positives by channel and by contract size, sizing the back-loaded-auction and single-asset risks
Q4How should a contract count in rollups by channel? Open question OQ-6 in the PRD.Both attribution options built side by side, one chosen with a reason
Q5Can questions be answered from the governed fields alone?A fixed set of about 30 questions, with the four from discovery, paraphrases and out-of-scope ones, and a pass rate
Q6Is the morning list what Ops needs? Open question OQ-4 in the PRD.A feedback session with Maria on the list and David on the rollups

2. What it builds, and what it deliberately skips

The prototype covers the parts of the PRD that test the bet. It skips the parts that make a product dependable in production. Those are Engineering's to build, and listing them here is what keeps the prototype throwaway.

Builds
  • A simulated book of contracts, assets and sales, calibrated to the Phase 2 book, including late-selling auction contracts and single-asset contracts
  • Every calculated field in the PRD, under the same names
  • Flag history replayed day by day, and the backtest and go-live report built on it
  • The ranked morning list and the Finance rollups
  • Plain-language questions that can read only the governed fields
Skips
  • Landing the three real source systems
  • Cross-source reconciliation
  • Reproducibility guarantees and scheduling
  • Access control
  • The weekly success scorecard

3. Plan

Timeboxed to five steps. Each step ends with something reviewable; the prototype stops at step five whether or not every question has a clean answer, because an unclear answer is itself a finding.

4. Done when

  • The go-live report states, for each of the five criteria, whether it is met and by how much.
  • The flag settings for the pilot are chosen from the sweep, not assumed.
  • The question layer answers every in-scope question in the evaluation set correctly and declines every out-of-scope one.
  • At least one change goes back into the PRD, recorded below with its reason.
  • A short recorded walkthrough shows the morning list, a rollup and a few questions answered.
